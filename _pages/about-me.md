I am a postdoctoral researcher at the Paris Interdisciplinary Energy Research Institute (LIED/PIERI, UMR 8236), Université Paris Cité / CNRS, working with José Halloy. My project, funded by the PEPR "Recyclage" programme (France 2030), is about reconstructing and modelling electricity distribution networks: assembling geospatial data, inferring scaling laws and estimating network topology.

I did my PhD in theoretical and numerical physics at CEA Paris-Saclay (IRAMIS/SPEC, SPHYNX group), supervised by Sébastien Aumaître and Hervé Bercegol, and funded by the CEA FOCUS TIME programme. My thesis, *Statistical physics and resource investments in networks for the energy transition*, was defended on 8 October 2025. It dealt with the statistical physics of large empirical networks: network reconstruction, generative growth models, and the optimisation of processes on network structures.

My path to networks is a little unusual. I came to them from quantum gravity: during an internship at the Raman Research Institute in Bangalore, I worked on sequential growth models in causal set theory, and I later turned that formalism towards the questions I find most urgent, those of the energy transition. Before that, I studied theoretical physics at École Normale Supérieure (ICFP Master's), high-energy physics at École Polytechnique, and physics in the Magistère programme of Université Paris Cité, with internships in post-Newtonian gravitation (Institut d'Astrophysique de Paris) and image analysis for the Euclid mission (APC, Paris). I also took a year out of my physics studies to follow the LOPHISS-SPH Master's programme in philosophy and history of science at Université Paris Diderot.

In 2026 I obtained the French national qualification for associate professor (CNU sections 28 and 29).

I teach physics at undergraduate level (see [Teaching](/teaching/)) and enjoy explaining science to wider audiences (see [Outreach](/outreach/)). The full details are in my [CV](/cv/).
