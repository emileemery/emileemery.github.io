My work applies statistical physics and complex network theory to the material infrastructures of the energy transition, with excursions into social and ecological systems.

Power grids as complex networks
------
How does the structure of a transmission grid reflect the territory it serves, and how will it need to change as electricity production shifts to renewables? I analyse real grids, starting with the French one, as spatial complex networks, and develop generative models that reproduce their growth from the differentiation between rural and urban areas.

Material stocks and resource investment
------
I study how the length and material content of electricity networks scale with population density across countries, and estimate the resources needed to deploy and renew long-lived energy grids worldwide. At LIED, this extends to low- and middle-voltage networks at international scale.

Energy storage in transmission grids
------
Where should storage be placed in a transmission network to minimise energy losses? I address this question as an optimisation problem on the grid graph.

Generative models for spatial networks
------
Many real networks are embedded in space, with nodes distributed very unevenly. I work on generative models that account for inhomogeneous node distributions in spatial networks.

Beyond energy
------
* Higher-order and group interactions in social systems, with friend-of-friend generative models
* Time-varying interactions in ecological communities, and their effect on equilibrium and stability
* Network complexity of industrial metabolism
