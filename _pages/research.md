I work on complex networks through generative modelling: I grow networks from simple rules and compare them with large empirical data sets, in order to explain how real networks are shaped, how robust they are, and what they consume. My research falls into three areas.

Energy transition
------

### Power grids as complex networks
I modelled a full national transmission grid, the French one, from operator data. Its efficiency stems from the high-voltage mesh interconnecting thermal plants, which means that integrating diffuse renewable production will require topological adjustment.

### Generative growth models
Can simple rules reproduce the structure of a real grid? A sequential growth model with rural–urban differentiation, connecting to nearest neighbours in rural areas and by preferential attachment in cities, reproduces both the topology of the French transmission grid and its Joule losses. More generally, most spatial generative models assume that nodes are distributed uniformly; I am replacing this assumption with a self-similar distribution and testing it on urban, road, electrical and biological networks.

### Scaling laws and material stocks
Population density predicts network properties that the data do not record. Scaling laws calibrated on France predict grid length and material stock in 35 electrified countries, yielding national resource inventories. I am also deriving the scaling exponents of hierarchical distribution networks from first principles rather than fitting them, and confirming them on the French electricity and gas grids.

### Material metabolism of energy grids
Grids lock copper and aluminium into long-lived stocks, and require continuous renewal flows to keep standing. I follow these stocks and flows for worldwide grids up to 2100, under several population scenarios.

### Optimising processes on networks
Given a fixed grid topology, where should energy storage be placed? I site storage so that the energy it returns exceeds the energy invested in it.

Networks of interacting individuals
------
* **Ecological networks**: environmental variation makes the network of species interactions change over time. We measure how this reshuffling widens or narrows the conditions under which species coexist.
* **Social networks**: a generative model for higher-order social interaction data, based on a local friend-of-friend attachment rule.
* **Open data**: turning records of large-scale competitions into open network datasets, directed, weighted, temporal and higher-order.

Causal set quantum gravity
------
Causal set theory proposes that spacetime is discrete at the fundamental level, and aims to reconcile general relativity and quantum mechanics through the concept of causality. During my Master's internship at the Raman Research Institute (Bangalore, 2022), supervised by Sumati Surya, I studied how dynamics is built in this framework, with a focus on the sequential growth paradigm and on the construction of observables, from the classical case towards the quantum one. This is where my interest in growing networks began.
